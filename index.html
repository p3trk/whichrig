Data: Open-Meteo (hourly, averaged into 2-hour steps; gusts are the 2-hour maximum). These are modelled winds, not station readings, so real gusts and lulls on the lakes can differ. A day matches when, for at least one gear in use, the wind stays inside that gear's range, from a selected direction and at or above the minimal temperature, for the chosen number of consecutive hours inside the chosen time of day. Underlined cells are the ones that count. Gear ranges are rough estimates scaled from rider weight and size; adjust them to match your own experience.

How ranges are calculated

Total mass M = rider + gear (+ wetsuit). Foil take-off board speed vto = 27.2 × √(M / (CL × foil cm²)). Lowest wind to get foiling = Crig × Cboard × Cskill × √(M / rig m²) × (vto / 8)0.75 × η. Fin skips the foil term. Crig: windfoil 2.1, wingfoil 3.1, sail on fin 3.3. Cboard = (board litres ÷ rider kg ÷ 1.4)−0.15. Cskill: beginner 1.15, intermediate 1.0, advanced 0.85 on foil and 0.9 on fin.

Front wing shape sets CL, η and the stay-up ratio: low aspect 1.0 / 1.05 / 0.80, mid 0.9 / 1.00 / 0.75, high 0.8 / 0.96 / 0.70, race 0.7 / 0.93 / 0.65.

Top of the range is the lower of two limits. The rig limit is when the sail or wing is overpowered: 4.3 (windfoil), 5.5 (wingfoil) or 6.2 (fin) × √(M / rig m²). The foil limit is its comfortable top speed, about 2.25 × vto, taken as the same wind speed. Both top-end limits are estimates; type your own range to override.

Warnings: vto above 12 kt means the foil is small for your weight, below 5 kt it is very big and slow. Results below 8 kt are extrapolated and likely optimistic.
